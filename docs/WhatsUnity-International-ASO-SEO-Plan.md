# WhatsUnity: International ASO, SEO and Landing-Page Plan

Research date: 29 September 2026  
Scope: Google Play organic discovery, website search acquisition, landing-page conversion, and supporting AI-search discoverability. USA and UK first; a practical expansion path for continental Europe and other markets.

## 1. The decision

**Keep community at the heart of WhatsUnity. Change the language people discover it through.**

Recommended global category: **resident app**.  
Recommended product promise: **Your neighbors, building chats and home services. Together.**  
Recommended buyer explanation: **Connect resident conversations with maintenance, visitor access and building management in one app.**

“Residential operating system” can explain the architecture later. It should not carry the acquisition strategy. People do not need to search for the whole vision to discover a product that fulfills it: they can arrive through a building chat, an HOA communication problem or a maintenance workflow.

Do not remove chat, bury community, or redesign the resident navigation solely for SEO. The grouped services menu, posts, general chat and building chat are a coherent product surface. Marketing should show that simplicity and then explain the operational depth.

Terminology: ASO means app store optimization; SEO means web search optimization. This plan also covers AEO/GEO, often used for answer-engine or generative-search visibility. They share useful content and product clarity, but have different measurement and distribution mechanisms.

### What the research can and cannot establish

- Public search results and official product pages establish relevant market vocabulary and competing offers. They do **not** establish monthly keyword volume, ranking difficulty or the number of searches in Google Play.
- Priorities below are judgments about product fit and intent, not measured traffic forecasts. No search-volume or conversion figures have been invented.
- Reviewed: live homepage HTML, the linked production JavaScript bundle, sitemap, selected official competitor pages/store listings, official Google guidance, and the resident section of your catalog PDF.
- The website search renderer could not open WhatsUnity, but direct HTTP retrieval returned its HTML, JavaScript and sitemap. This is **not evidence that Google cannot index the site**. No Search Console, field performance data or rendered visual audit was available.
- The public searches did not establish an exact WhatsUnity Play listing/package ID. Current production availability, countries, store metadata and search performance remain unverified. Prior internal-testing status must not be assumed current.
- Catalog descriptions establish intended/documented features, not independent evidence of deployment, capacity or reliability. Marketplace, shared public incidents and refined notification handling were described by you as forthcoming; do not market them as shipped.

## 2. Who should discover WhatsUnity, and what should happen next?

| Audience | Typical need | Search destination | Conversion |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resident in an onboarded property | Find the correct app and join neighbors | Play listing or resident page | Install/open → verify home → join building |
| Resident in an unsupported property | Replace fragmented building groups | Problem guide or resident page | Register building interest; receive a shareable manager introduction |
| HOA/condo board member | Improve communication and coordinate shared decisions | US solution page | Request demo/pilot |
| UK managing agent or residents’ association | Connect residents and manage building issues | UK solution page | Request demo/pilot |
| Property/community manager | Coordinate residents, security and maintenance | Buyer and workflow pages | Qualified demo → property launch |

**Do not optimize only for downloads.** An install from someone whose building cannot use the product may produce no value. A property that successfully onboards residents can create many useful installs.

Two acquisition paths should therefore coexist:

1. Search → qualified board/manager → pilot property → resident invitations → verified active households.
2. Search → interested resident → existing building join, or a clear “Bring WhatsUnity to my building” route.

Whether residents can create a building themselves is a product decision to confirm before writing onboarding promises. If management approval is required, say so before installation.

### Do not make these primary traffic targets

- “Community,” “chat,” “social network”: ambiguous; many users want unrelated social experiences.
- “Property management software”: broad expectations around rent, leases, accounting and portfolios. Use specific qualifiers and a capability matrix.
- “Apartments for rent,” “homes for sale,” “plumber near me”: consumer listing/service intent that the current launch may not fulfill.
- “Operational OS,” “RBC,” “Clean Architecture,” “SQLite,” “Appwrite”: architectural terms, not the main resident/buyer acquisition language.
- “Free,” “unlimited,” “best,” “number one”: do not add unless the claim is accurate and appropriate. Avoid unsupported superiority language.

### Validate demand before scaling content

For each country/language, export or record: phrase, language, market, source/date, estimated web volume/range if available, intent, top-result types, product fit, proposed URL, and eventual qualified conversion.

1. Use Keyword Planner or an established keyword dataset for **web** estimates; label ranges and data dates. Never label web volume as Google Play volume.
2. Inspect country/language-specific Google and Play results on representative devices. Record autocomplete as a suggestion, not volume evidence.
3. Use Search Console for actual website impressions and queries; Play Console for available acquisition/search breakdowns. Respect reporting thresholds; absent data is not proof of zero demand.
4. Score intent fit and service readiness before volume. A small board-level query can be more valuable than broad consumer traffic.
5. Start with five clusters. Expand when pages receive relevant impressions, leads or pilot conversations. Consolidate pages that compete for the same intent.

- Confirm the exact package ID, public listing URL, production/open-testing status, target-country availability and supported devices.

### 5.5 Localization, category and experiments

- Start with English and Arabic listings. Use American “neighbors” and British “neighbours” in their respective copy.
- Consider Lifestyle as a starting category for the resident-focused product, subject to the current Console options and actual app functionality. Do not choose Business merely because managers pay, or Social merely because chat exists.
- Google supports custom listings by country, search terms and custom URLs, among other audiences [S15]. Search targeting changes the shown listing; it does not guarantee ranking for those terms.
- Maintain one US and one UK message initially. Custom listings require deliberate translation handling. Check the Console's supported targeting combinations before implementing overlapping segments.
- Use Play listing experiments where supported [S16]: first screenshot, then short description, then other creative. Change one main hypothesis at a time. Do not assume every metadata field is available in the experiment tool.
- With low traffic, a few installs are not a reliable winner. Use Console confidence information and retain a baseline; sequential title changes are confounded by seasonality and other changes.
- Measure listing conversion together with verified-home activation and retention. Request honest reviews at a sensible point after users experience value; never buy reviews or filter prompts only to happy users.

## 7. Landing-page replacement plan

### Navigation

Logo → Product → For Residents → For Managers → Pricing → language selector → **Book a demo**. Provide a distinct **Resident login** link. Use normal links for pages, not only buttons opening modals.

### Hero copy

Eyebrow: **The resident app for connected buildings**

H1: **Your neighbors, building chats and home services. Together.**

Subheading:

> Chat with your neighbors, take part in building decisions and track maintenance in one place. WhatsUnity connects residents with the people who help their community run.

Primary CTA: **Bring WhatsUnity to your building**  
Secondary CTA: **See how it works**  
Existing-user link: **Already invited? Open WhatsUnity**

If the main commercial path is manager-led, the primary CTA opens a short demo form. Collect role, country, property size and contact details; avoid a long procurement form for first contact.

Hero visual: real home, building chat and maintenance status screens. Keep the familiar top shortcuts visible. Show useful readable UI rather than operational architecture diagrams.

### Page order and section content

1. **Hero: what it is, who it is for, next step.** Answer these in the first screen.
2. **A familiar problem:** “Building chat in one place. Maintenance updates somewhere else. Important decisions lost between them.” Three compact examples, not a generic enterprise pain statement.
3. **Community first:** posts, general/building conversations and polls. Explain that people can simply talk; structure is available when useful.
4. **Conversations connected to action:** demonstrate an existing maintenance journey: submit → staff assignment → progress → resolution. Only show attached conversations if shipped. Label the shared elevator incident concept as future work if mentioned.
5. **Everything stays relevant to your home:** explain building/apartment context through concrete examples. Avoid the technical phrase “identity graph.”
6. **For the people running your building:** three focused cards for management, maintenance and security, linked to deeper workflow pages.
7. **Household pricing:** explain who pays, what is included, minimum property commitments, fees and trial/pilot terms. “One home, one subscription” belongs here if it is the actual offer.
8. **Evidence:** real pilot screenshots, an authorized testimonial, a measured workflow improvement, or a clearly labeled demonstration. No fabricated logos, reviews or adoption counts.
9. **Getting started:** explain registration, verification, property approval and supported access methods.
10. **FAQ + closing CTA:** resolve access, language, availability, pricing and support questions.

### Suggested page metadata

Homepage title: `Resident App for Building Chat & Maintenance | WhatsUnity`

Homepage description: `Bring building chats, resident polls and maintenance updates together. WhatsUnity connects neighbors and the teams managing their community.`

US solution title: `HOA & Condo Communication App | WhatsUnity`

UK solution title: `Resident App for Blocks of Flats | WhatsUnity`

Descriptions are written for relevance and click-through; Google may choose different snippets [S18]. Character counts alone do not determine presentation or ranking.

### FAQ questions to answer visibly

- What is WhatsUnity, and who can use it?
- Can I join if my building has not signed up?
- How does building verification work?
- What is the difference between general chat and building chat?
- Who can see my contact information?
- Which maintenance updates can I track?
- Are resident polls advisory or usable for formal decisions?
- What does the household subscription include, and who pays?
- Which countries, languages and platforms are supported today?
- What works without an internet connection?

Write the offline answer per feature. Locally available data or gate verification does not mean disconnected devices can exchange live messages or receive new revocations immediately.

## 9. Technical SEO acceptance criteria

Use the existing application stack where practical; a framework migration is not automatically necessary.

- Public marketing URLs return useful status codes, readable page content, a descriptive title and accurate description. Prerendering/static generation is a practical delivery option, not a claim that JavaScript cannot rank [S19].
- Locale pages are directly accessible without changing browser settings. Prefer `/en/` and `/ar/`; query parameters are not inherently invalid if implemented consistently.
- Each genuinely translated page has the correct self-canonical and reciprocal hreflang references. Use language/region values such as `en-US` and `en-GB` when appropriate; do not create a fictional Europe language locale [S20].
- Check old query-language routes before migration; redirect their actual equivalents and update internal links. Do not redirect every old URL blindly to the homepage.
- Sitemap includes canonical, indexable URLs with meaningful change dates. Verify robots access and Search Console URL Inspection. A `site:` search is only a rough diagnostic.
- Keep marketing and help pages crawlable. Resident chats, identities, addresses and staff dashboards remain private; their content is not an SEO inventory. Authentication is the access control, not robots.txt.
- Schema describes the same truthful product and offer as visible content. Remove stack names from end-user software requirements. Do not add invented ratings or reviews.
- Do not allocate work to FAQ rich-result tactics: Google's current update log says the feature stopped appearing in May 2026 [S21]. Keep FAQs for people and useful answers.
- Resize and compress screenshots, set image dimensions, load noncritical videos on demand, and limit font families/weights. Measure mobile field/lab performance before choosing larger optimizations.
- Verify keyboard navigation, language direction, contrast, readable captions and reduced-motion behavior. English and Arabic pages must both work.
- Verify demo submission, resident open/join links, privacy links and Play download links on mobile. A working conversion path matters more than another technical metadata file.

## 10. AEO / AI-search discoverability

Your source already includes Markdown alternates, llms.txt links, schema and agent-discovery code. More of these is not the first priority.

Google's current guidance connects AI-search performance with ordinary search fundamentals; its June 2026 clarification says llms.txt is not needed for Google visibility [S21–S22]. This does not establish how every other assistant uses such files.

Recommended work:

1. Publish a concise factual “What is WhatsUnity?” answer on an accessible HTML page.
2. Maintain a current availability/features table separating shipped, property-configured and planned functionality.
3. Answer specific buyer questions with screenshots, examples and dates.
4. Publish an evidence-based case study when a pilot produces results.
5. Keep product facts consistent across site, Play, catalog and public profiles.
6. Retain machine-readable documentation if useful, but do not let it replace human-readable product pages.

Track identifiable referral traffic where available. For qualitative checks, record a fixed set of prompts, date, market, cited pages and answer accuracy. Assistant responses vary; a mention is not a reliable conversion metric or a guaranteed result of a formatting tactic.

## 12. Measurement and experiment plan

### Funnel definitions

| Stage | Metric | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|
| Discovery | Nonbrand impressions/clicks by page, query, country | Are the chosen terms reaching relevant people? |
| Intent | Qualified demo requests / relevant organic sessions | Does the page attract actual buyers? |
| Store | Store visitors → acquisitions, by available source/locale | Does the listing persuade? |
| Activation | Installed users → verified household memberships | Can new users access the product? |
| Adoption | Invited households → activated households by property | Does the community move together? |
| Value | Weekly active households; completed useful tasks | Are residents using it beyond installation? |
| Business | Pilots started, properties activated, retained properties | Is traffic creating a viable business? |

Proposed events: `demo_requested`, `demo_qualified`, `resident_open_clicked`, `store_outbound_clicked`, `building_interest_submitted`, `home_verified`, `first_useful_action`, `property_activated`.

Define “first useful action” consistently, such as participating in a conversation/poll or submitting a maintenance report. Track event types, not private message text, unit numbers or personal details in analytics URLs.

Do not equate a website store-button click with an attributed install. Reconcile website analytics, Play reports and actual onboarding separately. Separate branded searches from new nonbrand discovery.

### Tests in order

1. Hero clarity: can a new visitor explain what the app does, who can use it and the next step?
2. Resident-first hero versus manager-first hero on the appropriate audience pages.
3. First Play screenshot: familiar community conversation versus a combined home overview.
4. Short description: chat/decisions emphasis versus convenience/maintenance emphasis.
5. US buyer page: HOA communication language versus broader management language, measuring lead quality.

Establish baselines first. Choose evaluation windows based on actual traffic and buying cycles. No promised ranking, install total or statistical winner is implied by a 30-day calendar.
